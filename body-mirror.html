Reflection Exercise

The Body Mirror

An honest look at what you already know but haven't said out loud yet.

This is not a questionnaire. There are no right answers here, no framework to complete, no score at the end. This page is simply a place to write, without the audience of a spreadsheet, a coach, or a plan. What you write belongs to you. Nothing is sent anywhere.

So, be daring. Be honest. Be inventive, creative. Don't be afraid of words or thoughts you don't want to hear. Just write.

Start anywhere. The appointment you keep not making. The symptom you have stopped describing to anyone. The treatment you know is not working but haven't stopped. The conversation you've been meaning to have with someone. The part of your body you're quietly building your life around instead of addressing.

Some people spend five minutes and find one honest sentence. Others fill three pages and realize what they have been organizing their whole life around.

Write until you find the sentence you didn't plan to write. That's usually the one that matters.

If you'd rather do this by hand, print this page.

There's a reason handwriting surfaces things that typing doesn't. When you write by hand, your brain processes at a slower speed, which means your thoughts have to form more deliberately before they reach the page. The internal editor, the one that tidies your thinking before it becomes visible, has less time to intervene.

Studies on expressive writing consistently show that handwriting produces more emotionally honest and specific output than typing. The friction is the point. It slows you down enough to catch what you were about to skip over.
